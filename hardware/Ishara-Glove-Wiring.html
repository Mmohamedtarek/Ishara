Board pins

14 pins · 7 per side2 × 22 pins · J1 left, J3 right

Gold pads are used, dim pads stay empty. Pin 1 is the top-left pin with the USB connector facing up (XIAO) or antenna end up (DevKit).

Flex sensors

5 × voltage divider

Each finger is a flex sensor on top (to 3V3) and a fixed resistor below it (to GND). The middle point, the node, goes to the ADC pin. Bending raises the flex resistance, so the node voltage goes down. The flex sensor has no polarity.

Voltages under each finger are what a multimeter should read on the node, flat → fully bent. Optional: a 100 nF capacitor from each node to GND smooths the reading.

MPU-6050 and battery sense

I²C 0x68 · divider ÷2

Power

charger built into the XIAOTP4056 + AP2125N LDO

TP4056 modules ship with a 1.2 kΩ R_PROG (1 A charging), too much for a 600 mAh cell. Swap it for 2.7 kΩ to charge at about 445 mA. The AP2125N has no enable pin, so it is always on: only 3 connections. Bought at UGE Electronics (Egypt) with the SOT23 breakout adapter.

V_BAT is taken after the switch, so the sense divider draws nothing while the glove is off.

"Node" is the joint between a flex sensor and its fixed resistor. On a breadboard, it's the row where both legs meet.

Build order

one part at a time
  1. Board only. Before powering, check with the multimeter's beep mode that 3V3 and GND are not connected. Flash the firmware over USB and open the Serial Monitor. MPU-6050 FAIL is expected at this point.
  2. MPU-6050. Wire the 5 MPU wires. Reset the board: the banner should say MPU-6050 OK. Tilt it and watch roll change.
  3. One flex sensor (index). Wire its divider and watch the Index value in the status line change as you bend it.
  4. The other four fingers. Same divider each time; the thumb uses the 47 kΩ resistor.
  5. Battery sense. Two 220 kΩ resistors and the 100 nF capacitor.
  6. Battery, switch, TP4056 (with the 2.7 kΩ R_PROG) and AP2125N. Unplug USB first. Check Li-Po polarity twice before soldering.
  7. Calibrate. Type c and follow the steps, exactly as in the simulator.

Real flex sensors vary. A node that doesn't move when you bend the finger means a broken sensor or a loose wire; the firmware reports it as a range under 300.

Don't

  • Don't connect the Li-Po reversed. There is no reverse-polarity protection.
  • Don't leave the switch ON while the DevKit's USB cable is plugged in: two 3.3 V sources would fight.
  • Don't use an AMS1117, LD1117 or HT7533 instead of the AP2125N. The first two need more than 4.3 V in, and the HT7533 only gives 100 mA.
  • Don't solder wires straight onto the AP2125N. Use the SOT23 breakout adapter; the glove moves and bare SMD legs break.
  • Don't move signals to other pins without updating include/config.h. Avoid GPIO 0, 45, 46 (boot straps) and 19, 20 (USB).
  • Don't bend a flex sensor at its connector end. Reinforce that end with heat shrink.
  • Don't power the MPU-6050 from 5V. There is no 5V on battery.
